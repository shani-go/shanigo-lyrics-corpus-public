# Rights record — song_26

title:            يا حمامة الوادي (Ya Hamamit il-Wadi / "O Dove of the Valley")
                  — a Jerusalem lullaby
author/lyricist:  anonymous / traditional (Palestinian; collected near Jerusalem
                  from a woman named Zmikna, published 1901)
composer:         traditional (anonymous lullaby); NOT committed (lyrics only)
language:         ar (Palestinian Arabic — Jerusalem colloquial); the SHAAMI
                  (Levantine) dialect fixture
register:         dialect — Levantine colloquial (Jerusalem), lullaby register
edition:          TWO FILES. song_26.translit.txt is the VERBATIM source text:
                  Gustaf Dalman's Latin transliteration of 1901, letter for
                  letter as printed (his system: ā ē ī ō ū long vowels, ḥ ḍ ḫ
                  dotted emphatics, ǧ = ج, sch = ش, ch = خ, ' = ع/hamza).
                  song_26.txt is an ARABIC-SCRIPT RENDERING of that
                  transliteration made by this project in 2026 — NOT a published
                  Arabic text: spelling follows common written Levantine
                  conventions (هالباشا, لبّسوه, لايق), one line per printed line
                  (10 lines). Renderings flagged at review (decision 2026-09-29
                  in notes; unchanged): line 3/5
                  هليلي لو for halilīlu (Dalman: halilī is a pet word for small
                  children + "to him"); line 6 وفضّي لي for ufeḍīli (Dalman
                  glosses "spread wide for me [your feathers]"); line 2 للولادي
                  for lil'ulādi. Dalman's German translation is not committed.
genre:            traditional lullaby (Schlummerlied) — the Arabic set's first
                  children's / cradle song

notes:            WHY A RENDERING: no pre-1929 Arabic-script print of a
                  Levantine dialect song was found online (Dalman, Stephan,
                  Littmann, Jaussen, Lane and Spitta all print songs in
                  transliteration; the 1898–1908 al-Machriq volumes carry no
                  folk-song texts; nothing on ar.Wikisource). The developer
                  allowed a later Arabic rendering provided it does not change
                  the copyright consideration, which it does not (anonymous
                  text; PD witness; project-made conversion). The
                  transliteration file is the pinned witness; the Arabic file is
                  the working text and is marked as such. No modern
                  Arabic-script attestation of this exact lullaby was found to
                  cross-check spelling. Values screen: NEUTRAL — a mother
                  lulling a child, blessing him with fine clothes.
                  FOLKLORE REGIMES (not copyright, flagged for the record):
                  several Arab states vest state-held rights in expressions of
                  national folklore that never expire — e.g. Jordan's 1992 law
                  Art. 7 (the Ministry of Culture exercises the author's rights
                  over folklore), Egypt's 2002 law Art. 142, Morocco's 2000 law
                  Art. 7. They concern commercial exploitation inside those
                  states; no comparable regime applies in the US or the EU.
                  Recorded here because the song is anonymous folklore of the
                  Levant; the country of origin of a 1901 Jerusalem song is
                  itself an open question (Ottoman Palestine; today's
                  Palestinian territories; Jordanian law applied 1950–1967 in
                  the West Bank). DEVELOPER
