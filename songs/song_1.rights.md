title:            The John Brown Song (John Brown's Body / "Glory, Hallelujah")

author/lyricist:  William Weston Patton (1821-10-19 – 1889-12-31; US
                  abolitionist pastor) — the six verses ("The New John Brown
                  Song", written October 1861, printed in the Chicago Tribune
                  on 1861-12-16); the chorus and the "John Brown's body lies a
                  mould'ring in the grave / His soul is marching on" frame are
                  the communal, anonymous Union soldiers' song of spring 1861
                  (Fort Warren, Massachusetts). The earlier record's "Anonymous
                  / communal" described the song family, not this text — see
                  the correction in notes.

evidence:         The song originated in 1861 among Union soldiers early in the
                  American Civil War (the Fort Warren "Tiger" battalion; first
                  broadsides July 1861, e.g. C. S. Hall's, copyrighted
                  1861-07-16); the tune derives from the earlier hymn "Say,
                  Brothers, Will You Meet Us" (attributed to William Steffe).
                  The six literary verses in this file are William Weston

                  Tribune 1861-12-16 and in an uncredited Root & Cady sheet-music
                  edition (per Wikisource). Patton d. 1889-12-31 and publication
                  in 1861 ⇒ well before 1929, so public domain in the US and
                  worldwide by age. (Distinct from Julia Ward Howe's later
                  "Battle Hymn of the Republic", 1862 — Howe died 1910, itself
                  long past life+70.)
