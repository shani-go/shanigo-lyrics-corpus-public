                  FOLKLORE REGIMES (not copyright, flagged for the record):
                  several Arab states vest state-held rights in expressions of
                  national folklore that never expire — e.g. Jordan's 1992 law
                  Art. 7 (the Ministry of Culture exercises the author's rights
                  over folklore), Egypt's 2002 law Art. 142, Morocco's 2000 law
                  Art. 7. They concern commercial exploitation inside those
                  states; no comparable regime applies in the US or the EU.
                  Recorded here because the song is anonymous folklore of the
                  Levant; the country of origin of a 1922 Jerusalem song is
                  itself an open question (Ottoman Palestine; today's
                  Palestinian territories; Jordanian law applied 1950–1967 in
                  the West Bank). CROSS-CHECK

                  WITNESS NOTE CLOSED 2026-10-14 (Claude Code's research, at the
                  developer's request the same day): (1) Stephan's dates are
                  confirmed as 1894–1949 (VIAF 89579269; English Wikipedia
                  "Stephan Hanna Stephan": died in Lebanon in 1949), so the
                  Colombia / Equatorial Guinea term on his own writing does run
                  to 2029-12-31. (2) The committed files take none of his
                  writing: song_27.translit.txt is the anonymous song's words
                  as a singer gave them, written in a general transliteration
                  scheme, and song_27.txt adds only the Arabic-script spellings
                  of single words from his footnotes; his translation,
                  commentary and footnote prose are not committed. (3) A
                  transcriber's right needs an original creation of his own.
                  In Colombia the Andean Community's Decision 351 governs; its
                  Art. 3 defines a work as an original intellectual creation of
                  an artistic, scientific or literary nature, and the Andean
                  Tribunal treats originality as the primary requirement. The
                  words are the singer's, and a notation scheme is a method,
                  not expression, so the files reproduce no work of Stephan's.
                  Andean case law presumes the originality of a claimed work,
                  but there is no work of his here to presume it of: the files'
                  only content is another's words. Equatorial Guinea's statute
                  was not checked separately; the same originality reasoning is
                  expected of a civil-law copyright law. (A common-law
                  sweat-of-the-brow claim of the Walter v Lane kind would arise
                  only in life+70 countries, where his term ran out 2019-12-31.)
                  Conclusion: no residual exposure; both files may ship in a
                  public release. Reopen only if either country is shown to
                  protect a non-original transcription.
