## Texts dedicated or granted to the public domain

These texts were placed in the public domain, or made available under an
express, unconditional permission, by their rights holders. No condition from
this repository attaches to them. Attribution is given as a courtesy.

**song_29, song_30, song_31 — Tom Lehrer (1928–2025).** Lehrer relinquished
all copyright in his lyrics and music in a statement of October 2020 and a
disclaimer dated 2022-11-26, saying his songs are "now in the public domain".
Where the law does not let an author abandon copyright, the disclaimer's
express, irrevocable permission operates as a licence.

- Dedication: <https://tomlehrersongs.com/disclaimer/>. Archived copy:
  <http://web.archive.org/web/20230131061145/https://tomlehrersongs.com/disclaimer/>
  (viewed by the developer on 2026-10-14: it shows the 2022-11-26 disclaimer). The
  wording is quoted in `songs/song_29.rights.md` (`evidence:`).
- A second archived copy of the lyric sheets and their release statement:
  Wikimedia Commons, File:Tom Lehrer song lyrics (website).pdf (retrieved
  from the author's site on 2020-10-22), and, for the 2020 addendum to
  song_30, File:Tom Lehrer lyrics, Elements 103 to 118.pdf.
- Courtesy attribution: words by Tom Lehrer.
- **song_31 (Te Amo): authorship basis.** The author's sheet reads "words and
  music by Tom Lehrer (with thanks to Munro Edmonson)". The text is treated as
  Lehrer's alone, for three reasons. The credit line names Lehrer only, and
  Edmonson stands in a "with thanks to" parenthesis, which is the form of an
  acknowledgement. The author and the Tom Lehrer Trust 2000 published the
  sheet on their own account. No source found credits Edmonson with any part
  of the words. If that reading were wrong, Lehrer's grant would still cover
  use in the US, where a co-owner may grant non-exclusive licences. Outside
  the US, a hypothetical Edmonson share (Munro Edmonson, 1924–2002) would be
  unlicensed in jurisdictions that need every co-owner's consent, for as long
  as the joint work's term runs (to 2095-12-31 under life+70). If evidence of
  co-authorship turns up, the song is withdrawn from the release.

## Public-domain texts: notes for reuse

The Tier 0 texts are public domain. No rights are claimed in them and no
licence is needed. These notes concern regimes outside copyright, or a layer
of a source edition.

**song_4 — La Cucaracha.** Credit: traditional, Mexico, popularised in the
Revolution era, with Spanish antecedents (attested in 1819, 1859 and 1883).
**Mexican folklore law (not copyright):** since the reform published on
2020-01-24, Mexico's Ley Federal del Derecho de Autor, Arts. 157–160, protects
works of popular culture and traditional cultural expressions against
exploitation without the written authorisation of the people or community
that holds them. Where the holder is in doubt, Art. 160 routes a request for
use for profit through the Secretaría de Cultura. La Cucaracha is a national
folk song with Spanish antecedents rather than the expression of an
identified people or community, so the regime most likely does not reach it.
The regime concerns commercial exploitation inside Mexico.

**song_26 and song_27 — Palestinian traditional songs.** **Arab-state
folklore regimes (not copyright):** several Arab states vest state-held
rights in expressions of national folklore that never expire. Examples are
Jordan's 1992 law, Art. 7, under which the Ministry of Culture exercises the
author's rights over folklore; Egypt's 2002 law, Art. 142; and Morocco's 2000
law, Art. 7. These concern commercial exploitation inside those states, and
no comparable regime applies in the US or the EU. The country of origin of a
Jerusalem song collected in 1901 or 1922 is itself an open question: Ottoman
Palestine, today's Palestinian territories, or Jordanian law, which applied
in the West Bank from 1950 to 1967. In each song the Arabic file
(`song_<n>.txt`) is a project rendering of the collector's transliteration.
The transliteration itself is kept verbatim beside it as
`song_<n>.translit.txt` (Dalman 1901; Stephan 1922). The rendering layer is
waived under CC0 1.0, so this is a request, not a condition: if you
republish an Arabic file, please say that it is a rendering, not a
published Arabic text.

**song_25 — a Kuwaiti sawt by Abdullah al-Faraj (d. 1901).** The poem is
public domain everywhere. The file keeps the partial vocalization of the 2001
edition it was read from, and it is not established whether that layer is the
1919 first print's or was added in 2001. In the US, diacritics added to a
public-domain text are not original authorship. In the EU, Directive
2006/116/EC, Art. 5, lets a member state grant a related right in critical
and scientific editions of public-domain works, for at most 30 years from
publication. Germany's version (§ 70 UrhG) lasts 25 years. A 2001 layer could
therefore be claimed in Germany to 2026-12-31 at most, and nowhere in the EU
after 2031-12-31. That holds only if partial vocalization counts as a
critical edition and a Kuwaiti edition qualifies for protection there, and
both are doubtful. The file ships as is.
